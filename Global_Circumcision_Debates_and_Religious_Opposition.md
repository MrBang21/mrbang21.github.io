# Global Compendium: Legislative, Judicial, and Policy Debates on Banning Non-Therapeutic Male Circumcision vs. Faith-Based Counter-Ban Opposition

This document compiles every sovereign state, regional jurisdiction, and supranational body that has formally introduced, debated, litigated, or recommended criminal bans, moratoria, or statutory age minimums on non-therapeutic infant and minor male circumcision, paired with the legal, theological, and civil rights counter-ban opposition raised by Jewish and Muslim communities. Entries are ordered chronologically by the date of the first ban-related initiative in each jurisdiction, from oldest to newest.

No country currently maintains a nationwide criminal ban on non-therapeutic infant male circumcision. All major prohibition and age-minimum proposals either failed, were reversed by clarifying legislation or executive decrees, or remained non-binding recommendations.

## 1. Netherlands (First Event: May 27, 2010)

### Background and Ban Initiative
On May 27, 2010, the Royal Dutch Medical Association (*Koninklijke Nederlandsche Maatschappij tot bevordering der Geneeskunst* – KNMG) published an official policy viewpoint titled **"The non-therapeutic circumcision of male minors"** (*Niet-therapeutische circumcisie bij minderjarige jongens*), co-signed by major Dutch pediatric, urological, and surgical societies.

The KNMG declared that non-therapeutic circumcision of male minors violates a child's constitutional right to bodily integrity and autonomy, stating explicitly that *"there are good reasons for a statutory prohibition of non-therapeutic circumcision of male minors, as exists for female genital mutilation."* While expressing concern that an immediate criminal ban might drive the procedure underground, the KNMG urged a firm policy of deterrence and progressive phase-out, sparking parliamentary debates over statutory restriction.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Dutch-Jewish Community (*Nederlands-Israëlitisch Kerkgenootschap* – NIK) and the Muslim Coordination Organization (*Contactorgaan Moslims en Overheid* – CMO) mounted fierce counter-ban opposition. Religious leaders argued that equating foundational Abrahamic obligations with genital mutilation or child abuse stigmatized over a million Dutch citizens and threatened the legal viability of Jewish and Muslim family life in the Netherlands.

## 2. United States — San Francisco & California (First Event: May 2011)

### Background and Ban Initiative
In May 2011, anti-circumcision activists qualified a municipal ballot initiative for the November 2011 election in San Francisco, California (and gathered signatures for a parallel measure in Santa Monica). The proposed ordinance sought to criminally outlaw male circumcision on anyone under 18 within city limits—with no exemption for religious practice—punishable as a misdemeanor carrying up to one year in county jail and a $1,000 fine.

In July 2011, California Superior Court Judge Loretta Giorgi struck the initiative from the ballot, ruling that state law preempts municipalities from prohibiting lawful medical procedures. In October 2011, California Governor Jerry Brown signed **Assembly Bill 768 (AB 768)** into law, establishing statewide preemption and permanently barring any city, county, or local jurisdiction in California from banning or restricting male circumcision.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
A broad coalition—including the Anti-Defamation League (ADL), the American Jewish Committee (AJC), the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR), and the ACLU of Northern California—intervened in court and backed AB 768. They argued that a criminal ban directly violated the First Amendment's Free Exercise Clause and was designed to criminalize observant Jewish and Muslim parents and practitioners.

## 3. Norway (First Event: August 31, 2011)

### Background and Ban Initiatives
On August 31, 2011, Norwegian Children's Ombudsman (*Barneombudet*) Reidar Hjermann formally proposed introducing a statutory minimum age limit of 15 or 16 years for non-therapeutic male circumcision, effectively banning infant and childhood circumcision. His successor, Anne Lindboe, escalated the campaign across Scandinavia in 2012–2013, demanding a minimum age of 18 so boys could consent personally.

Political parties subsequently adopted explicit ban platforms: the Centre Party (*Senterpartiet*) backed an 18-year age minimum in 2012, and in May 2017, the Progress Party (*Fremskrittspartiet* – FrP)—then a member of Norway's governing coalition—voted at its annual convention to support a statutory ban on non-therapeutic circumcision for boys under 16. Parliament ultimately rejected an age-based ban.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Jewish Community in Oslo (*Det Mosaiske Trossamfund*) and the Islamic Council of Norway (*Islamsk Råd Norge*) warned that an age-based prohibition would effectively expel observant Jews and Muslims from Norway. Former community president Ervin Kohn noted that outlawing *Brit Milah* would make Norway the first European democracy since World War II to bar Jewish families from fulfilling a foundational covenantal rite.

## 4. Slovenia (First Event: February 2012)

### Background and De Facto Ban Ruling
In February 2012, the Human Rights Ombudsman of the Republic of Slovenia (*Varuh človekovih pravic*) issued a formal constitutional opinion titled *"Obrezovanje fantkov iz nemedicinskih razlogov je kršitev otrokovih pravic"* (Circumcision of boys for non-medical reasons is a violation of children's rights). The Ombudsman ruled that performing non-therapeutic circumcision on boys unable to give informed consent violates Article 35 (Personal Dignity and Safety) and Article 56 (Rights of Children) of the Slovenian Constitution, as well as the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child.

Following the Ombudsman's ruling and parallel opinions from the Slovenian Medical Chamber, public hospitals and state medical facilities across Slovenia halted elective ritual circumcisions, creating a de facto institutional prohibition.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Islamic Community in the Republic of Slovenia (*Islamska skupnost v Republiki Sloveniji*) and the Jewish Community of Slovenia condemned the ruling. Mufti Nedžad Grabus and Jewish community representatives warned that classifying religious circumcision as unlawful bodily injury violated constitutional freedom of religion and forced parents to travel abroad or turn to unregulated practitioners.

## 5. Germany (First Event: May 7, 2012)

### Background and Judicial Ban
On May 7, 2012, the Cologne Regional Court (*Landgericht Köln*, Az. 151 Ns 169/11) ruled that non-therapeutic religious circumcision of a minor constitutes unlawful bodily harm (*rechtswidrige Körperverletzung* under § 223 StGB). The court held that a child's constitutional right to bodily integrity and self-determination (Art. 2 Basic Law) overrides parental custody rights (Art. 6 Basic Law) and religious freedom (Art. 4 Basic Law).

The judgment triggered an immediate de facto ban across Germany as hospitals and physicians suspended ritual circumcisions out of fear of criminal prosecution, while 66 opposition MPs tabled a motion for a statutory minimum age of 14. On December 12, 2012, following intense domestic and international protests, the *Bundestag* overturned the judicial ban by enacting **§ 1631d of the German Civil Code (*BGB*)**, affirming parental authority to consent to medically sound male infant circumcision.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
German Jewish and Muslim leadership formed an unprecedented united front against the judicial ban. The Central Council of Jews in Germany, the Central Council of Muslims in Germany, and the Conference of European Rabbis warned that criminalizing circumcision made continued Jewish and Muslim life in Germany impossible.

### Direct Quotes
- **Rabbi Pinchas Goldschmidt (President, Conference of European Rabbis):**
  > *"Calling it the 'worst attack on Jewish life since the Holocaust,' the Conference of European Rabbis strongly condemned a recent German court decision... If the ruling is allowed to stand, then I don't see a future for Jews in Germany. It is an existential question for the Jewish community in Germany."*
- **Dieter Graumann (President, Central Council of Jews in Germany):**
  > *"I always readily carried this burden because I was firmly convinced that this country and these people deserved it. Now, for the first time my basic convictions are starting to shake. For the first time I feel resignation. I seriously ask if the country still wants us."*
- **Aiman Mazyek (Chairman, Central Council of Muslims in Germany – ZMD):**
  > Condemned the ruling as a *"blatant and inadmissible interference"* in fundamental religious freedom and parental autonomy, warning that criminalizing circumcision would alienate millions of law-abiding citizens and *"inevitably push families into unregulated, dangerous back-alley procedures or force them abroad to protect their faith."*

## 6. Austria (First Event: July 2012)

### Background and Regional Hospital Ban
In July 2012, immediately following the Cologne court judgment in Germany, the Governor of Vorarlberg, Markus Wallner, instructed state-run hospitals (*Landeskrankenhäuser*) to suspend all non-therapeutic circumcisions, imposing a regional clinical moratorium. Hospital authorities in Vienna and Styria likewise initiated reviews of surgical guidelines.

On July 31, 2012, the Austrian Federal Ministry of Justice (*Bundesministerium für Justiz*) ended the moratorium by issuing a formal decree (**GZ BMJ-LE12.0001/0003-IV 3/2012**) confirming that under § 90 Abs. 1 of the Austrian Criminal Code (*StGB*), religious circumcision performed with parental consent according to medical standards does not constitute criminal assault.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Jewish Community of Vienna (*Israelitische Kultusgemeinde Wien* – IKG) and the Islamic Religious Authority in Austria (*Islamische Glaubensgemeinschaft in Österreich* – IGGÖ) condemned the hospital ban as an unconstitutional infringement on religious freedom under the Austrian State Basic Law (*Staatsgrundgesetz*).

## 7. Switzerland (First Event: July 2012)

### Background and Hospital Moratorium
In July 2012, citing legal uncertainty triggered by the Cologne court ruling in Germany, the University Children's Hospital Zurich (*Kinderspital Zürich*)—followed by the Cantonal Hospital of St. Gallen—imposed a temporary ban/moratorium on performing non-therapeutic religious circumcisions, raising concern that doctors could face assault charges under the Swiss Criminal Code (*StGB*).

In September 2012, the Swiss Federal Office of Justice (*Bundesamt für Justiz* – BJ) issued a legal opinion confirming that parental proxy consent for religious circumcision remains valid under the Swiss Civil Code (*ZGB*) when performed safely with pain relief, leading hospitals to lift the suspension without statutory amendment.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Swiss Federation of Jewish Communities (*Schweizerischer Israelitischer Gemeindebund* – SIG) and the Federation of Islamic Organizations in Switzerland (FIOS) protested the clinical moratorium, warning that hospital bans infringed upon freedom of conscience and religion under Article 15 of the Swiss Federal Constitution.

## 8. Australia — Tasmania (First Event: August 21, 2012)

### Background and Statutory Ban Recommendation
On August 21, 2012, following a three-year inquiry, the Tasmania Law Reform Institute (TLRI) published **Final Report No. 17: Non-Therapeutic Male Circumcision**. The report concluded that non-therapeutic circumcision of young boys infringes upon a child's bodily integrity and recommended that the Tasmanian Parliament enact a new criminal offense generally prohibiting the circumcision of incapable minors (while recommending a narrow statutory exception for well-established religious or ethnicity-motivated circumcision). The Tasmanian government ultimately declined to enact the criminal prohibition.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
During the inquiry and following the report's release, the Executive Council of Australian Jewry (ECAJ), the Hobart Hebrew Congregation, and Australian Muslim organizations submitted strong counter-ban objections. Jewish leaders warned that criminalizing circumcision—or conditioning religious exemptions on intrusive state criteria—threatened the survival of Tasmania's historic Jewish community and undermined fundamental religious liberties.

## 9. Sweden (First Event: September 2013)

### Background and Ban Initiatives
In September 2013, Swedish Children's Ombudsman (*Barnombudsmannen*) Fredrik Malmberg and the Swedish Medical Association (*Läkarförbundet*) joined the joint Nordic declaration in Oslo demanding that governments outlaw non-therapeutic circumcision of boys until they are old enough to consent personally.

Parliamentary and party-level ban initiatives followed: in November 2014 (*Motion 2014/15:2118*) and September 2018 (*Motion 2018/19:35*, submitted by the Sweden Democrats / *Sverigedemokraterna*), MPs introduced motions in the *Riksdag* demanding a total statutory ban on non-medical circumcision of minors. In September 2019, the Centre Party (*Centerpartiet*) passed a national party congress resolution calling for a ban on non-medical circumcision of boys, before party leadership retreated amid intense domestic and international condemnation.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Official Council of Swedish Jewish Communities (*Judiska Centralrådet*) and Swedish Muslim federations denounced the ban motions and the Centre Party vote, declaring that a ban would effectively end organized Jewish life in Sweden and make Sweden the first Western democracy since World War II to outlaw core Jewish and Islamic practices.

## 10. Council of Europe — Supranational (First Event: October 1, 2013)

### Background and Parliamentary Action
On October 1, 2013, the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe (PACE) adopted **Resolution 1952 (2013)**, titled *"Children's right to physical integrity"*. Drafted by rapporteur Marlene Rupprecht (Germany), the resolution explicitly grouped *"the circumcision of young boys for religious reasons"* alongside female genital mutilation, non-consensual intersex surgeries, and forced tattoos or piercings, calling on member states to adopt legal provisions ensuring invasive procedures are not performed before a child can be consulted.

In March 2014, the Committee of Ministers rejected equating religious male circumcision with FGM, and in 2015 PACE adopted **Resolution 2076 (2015)** affirming the protection of religious freedom and clarifying that Resolution 1952 did not call for a ban.

### Jewish, Muslim, and Diplomatic Counter-Ban Responses
Resolution 1952 provoked immediate diplomatic condemnation from the Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), Turkey's Directorate of Religious Affairs (*Diyanet*), and European Jewish and Muslim councils, who denounced it as an assault on Article 9 of the European Convention on Human Rights (Freedom of Thought, Conscience, and Religion).

### Direct Quotes
- **Mehmet Görmez (President, Directorate of Religious Affairs / Diyanet, Turkey):**
  > *"No secular court or legal authority possesses the right to place foundational religious obligations on trial. Treating an essential tenet of Abrahamic religions—practiced safely for millennia—as a criminal assault is a manifestation of an aggressive secular fundamentalism that seeks to eradicate Islamic identity from Europe."*
- **The European Council for Fatwa and Research (ECFR):**
  > Banning circumcision constitutes *"an assault on religious heritage and parental rights that threatens the basic conditions of coexistence between religious minorities and Western states."*

## 11. Finland (First Event: 2014)

### Background and Legislative Ban Proposals
While Finnish Supreme Court precedents (**KKO 2008:93** and **KKO 2016:24**) held that medically proper ritual circumcision with parental consent is not criminal assault, MPs in the Finnish Parliament (*Eduskunta*) repeatedly introduced proposals to criminalize non-medical circumcision of boys—most notably through Legislative Initiative **LA 41/2014 vp** and during November 2020 parliamentary committee debates (*LaVM 6/2020 vp* / Plenary Debate *PTK 139/2020 vp*) on criminalizing Female Genital Mutilation.

Backed by the Finnish Medical Association (*Suomen Lääkäriliitto*), proponents sought to amend the Penal Code to outlaw non-therapeutic circumcision of minor boys alongside FGM. Parliament ultimately rejected including male circumcision due to constitutional protections of religious freedom.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Central Council of Jewish Communities in Finland (*Suomen Juutalaisten Seurakuntien Keskusneuvosto*) and the Islamic Council of Finland (*Suomen Islamilainen Neuvosto*) opposed the criminalization amendments, warning that prohibiting infant circumcision would extinguish Finland's historic Jewish community and violate constitutional religious rights.

## 12. Belgium (First Event: May 2017)

### Background and Bioethics Ban Call / Prosecutions
In May 2017 (published September 2017), the Belgian Federal Advisory Committee on Bioethics (*Comité consultatif de Bioéthique de Belgique*) issued **Advisory Opinion No. 70 (*Avis n° 70*)**, concluding unanimously that non-medical circumcision of male minors violates a child's bodily integrity and autonomy, and calling on the government to re-evaluate whether the practice should remain legal without personal consent and to end public health insurance reimbursement.

Tensions escalated sharply in 2025–2026 when Antwerp prosecutors opened criminal investigations and conducted police raids against ritual circumcisers (*mohelim*) for practicing without medical licenses, triggering an international battle over de facto criminalization of *Brit Milah* in Belgium.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Coordinating Committee of Jewish Organizations in Belgium (CCOJB), the European Jewish Association (EJA), and Belgian Muslim leaders condemned both Opinion No. 70 and the Antwerp prosecutions as a direct violation of Article 9 of the European Convention on Human Rights and a de facto ban on Jewish life in Belgium.

### Direct Quotes
- **Open Letter from European Jewish Leaders to the Governments of Europe, Belgium, and EU Institutions (Rabbi Menachem Margolin, EJA):**
  > *"We remind Belgium that Freedom of religion is a fundamental right. This decision stands in direct contradiction to it. **The message being sent here is clear: Jews are no longer welcome in Belgium. And Belgian Jews are now second-class citizens with limited rights.** ... All the words of politicians about the importance of Jewish life in Europe mean nothing if they do not act immediately to stop this injustice. We urge them and the Belgian Government to stop this madness. Circumcision is not a crime."*
- **Rabbi Menachem Margolin on Legal Prosecutions & Measures:**
  > *"Another red line has been crossed. This prosecution is antisemitic in nature. Not borderline, not ambiguous, but antisemitic. It is a clear attempt to misuse irrelevant constitutional provisions in order to effectively ban circumcision, in a manner reminiscent of efforts taken in Europe against Jewish practice prior to the Second World War. Not similar. Not comparable. Identical in its logic of restriction."*

## 13. Iceland (First Event: February 2018)

### Background and Legislative Ban Bill
In February 2018, Member of Parliament Silja Dögg Gunnarsdóttir (Progressive Party) and cross-party co-sponsors introduced **Bill 183 (148th Legislative Session, Case 114)** in the Icelandic Parliament (*Alþingi*). The bill proposed amending Article 218 of the Icelandic General Penal Code (*Almenn hegningarlög nr. 19/1940*) to criminalize non-therapeutic circumcision of boys under 18 years of age under the same statutory framework as Female Genital Mutilation, punishable by up to six years in prison.

Following intense domestic committee scrutiny and coordinated international diplomatic and interfaith opposition, the Judicial Affairs and Education Committee recommended dismissing the bill, and it lapsed without reaching a final plenary vote.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
Nordic Jewish communities, the European Jewish Association (EJA), the European Jewish Congress (EJC), and Icelandic Muslim leaders condemned Bill 183, emphasizing that because *Halakha* mandates *Brit Milah* on the eighth day of life, an 18-year age minimum would permanently bar observant Jewish life in Iceland and set an anti-pluralist precedent across Europe.

### Direct Quotes
- **Joint Nordic Jewish Communities (Official Letter to the Alþingi, 2018):**
  > *"Banning Brit Milah will be an effective deterrent and will guarantee that no Jewish community will ever be established in Iceland. ... The reason this concerns us is that you are about to attack Judaism in a way that concerns Jews all over the world. You will be the only country to ban one of the most central rites of our religion."*
- **Rabbi Menachem Margolin (Chairman, European Jewish Association):**
  > *"Iceland would make itself the first modern democracy to outlaw a foundational Jewish practice. It sends an undeniable signal of intolerance that will isolate Iceland internationally and bar Jewish families from ever calling Iceland their home."*

## 14. Denmark (First Event: June 2018)

### Background and Legislative Ban Initiative
In June 2018, Danish activist group *Intakt Danmark* launched national citizens' initiative ***Borgerforslag FT-00124*** titled *"Indførelse af 18 års mindstealder for omskæring af raske børn"* (Introduction of an 18-year minimum age for circumcision of healthy children). After gathering over 54,000 verified signatures, the proposal was formally tabled in the Danish Parliament (*Folketinget*) as **Resolution B 7** (2020–2021), proposing to amend the Danish Penal Code (§ 245a) to punish non-therapeutic circumcision of minors with up to six years in prison.

While supported by the Danish Medical Association (*Lægeforeningen*), major governing and opposition parties—including the Social Democrats, Liberals, and Conservatives—opposed the measure, warning of severe diplomatic, security, and minority-rights consequences. In May 2021, the *Folketing* voted decisively against the ban.

### Jewish and Muslim Counter-Ban Responses
The Danish Jewish Community (*Det Jødiske Samfund i Danmark*) and Danish Muslim organizations formed an unprecedented united coalition against the proposal. Community leaders characterized the bill as the gravest existential threat to Danish Jewry since World War II, warning that an 18-year age minimum would extinguish Jewish life in Denmark and drive families to seek procedures abroad or underground.

### Direct Quotes
- **Henri Goldstein (President, Det Jødiske Samfund i Danmark):**
  > *"The legislation is the worst threat since World War II as far as Danish Jews are concerned. Circumcision is absolutely essential for being Jewish... It is an existential threat to our continued existence in this country."*
- **Dan Rosenberg Asmussen (Former Chairman, Det Jødiske Samfund i Danmark):**
  > *"If a ban is introduced in Denmark, it would mean that Jewish life in Denmark is completely closed down. You cannot live a Jewish life without circumcising your boys on the eighth day. It is an ultimatum."*
